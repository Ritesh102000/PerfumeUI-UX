A stain can start
the conversation.

Homeowners often notice a roof in pieces: a mark on the ceiling, a shingle seen from the driveway, a change after rain. Those details are worth bringing up. You do not need to name the problem before you ask about an inspection.

Find a starting point
THREE QUESTIONS TO KEEP APART

What you saw.
What can be seen.
What comes next.

They are related, but they are not the same answer. Keep each one clear as you talk about the roof.

01 / THE FIRST CLUEWhat changed?

Say where you noticed it and when. A description from the ground or inside the house is enough to begin; there is no need to climb onto the roof.

Explore common concerns
02 / THE VISITWhat could be seen?

Before arranging a visit, confirm the property area, access, inspection scope and any fee. Afterward, ask which areas were actually observed and which remain out of view.

Understand the visit
03 / THE DECISIONWhat is being proposed?

If work is suggested, ask for the area involved, materials, scope, price inclusions and timing in writing. You should have the details before you decide.
